Event horizon

At r = 2GM/c², escape would need the speed of light. For this hole that is –. Nothing that crosses it comes back, not even light.

Shadow and photon ring

At 1.5 times the horizon radius, light itself can orbit, but not stably. The dark disc you see is the shadow: about 2.6 times wider than the horizon, because light passing that close gets pulled in. The thin bright line around it is light that looped around the hole before reaching you.

Innermost stable orbit

Closer than 3 horizon radii (6 GM/c²), no circular orbit survives a nudge. The disk ends there and gas spirals straight in. Aim something just inside it and watch it go.

Doppler beaming

The disk gas orbits at up to half the speed of light. The side coming toward you looks bluer and far brighter, and the side moving away looks dim and red. Light from behind the hole is bent over the top and bottom, which is why the disk seems to wrap around the shadow.

Spaghettification

Gravity pulls harder on your near side than your far side. For a 10 solar mass hole this shreds a cow thousands of kilometres out. For Sgr A* a cow crosses the horizon intact, but a star is still torn into a stream of gas. For M87* even stars are swallowed whole.

Redshift and frozen time

Seen from far away, clocks near the horizon slow toward zero and light stretches to the red. Falling objects dim, redden and seem to stall at the edge. For the object itself, nothing special happens at the crossing.
